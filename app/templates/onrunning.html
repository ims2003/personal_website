On Running

I’m writing this in November of 2026, for close to 5 months now I have been focusing my training almost entirely on running for the first time in roughly 2 years. The point of putting my thoughts into words this time around is to capture what it has felt like to return to running and the effects it has had on me both physically and psychologically in rather turbulent periods, an uptick in my time spent reading has also compelled me to put these thoughts into written words. Due to a lingering injury regarding the cartilage in my right shoulder I’m haven’t been able to compete and train in triathlon, spending my summer working abroad doubled down on that by leaving me stranded without a bike. When these things first came up in June of 2026 I took it as an opportunity to keep fit by doing some more running than I had been and sign up for a half marathon with my friends, James, Matthew, Riley, and Mackenzie.

Thus starting in late June of 2026, under the hot mediterranean sun of central Spain I began running again.

In all honesty the summer was not impressive, compared to my younger years I was only running a fraction of what I used to, the pace at which I did them would be deemed snail like by a young me. I was never much of a race horse anyways. All of my runs were done alone at ungodly hours in the morning or under the ungodly heat of the sun in a notoriously warm summer in Europe. Despite the loneliness and, for lack of a better word, angst regarding my time in Europe it may be the calmest period as of late, reflected in the relaxed nature of the training. I averaged 57 kilometres per week for 8 weeks.

As the summer turned to fall and I returned to North America, training took a turn. To be completely honest I had nothing except training at the time, I had a couple weeks between work terms, I was away from my loved ones and losing others. With nothing else to distract myself with, it seemed natural to train more, it was easier than to not. With my half marathon in the not so distant horizon, intensity and volume ticked up. My body reflected the change in habits with sunken eyes and thinning out the most it had in 2 years. Rather quickly 50 kilometre weeks turned into 100 kilometre weeks with over 200 kilometres of biking on top.

The uptick in cycling volume comes from taking an opportunity to race a local Fondo as my power per kilogram numbers reached an all time high, despite the measly 189k ridden throughout the summer months (55k of them a hellish experience in the Sierra Nevada), crazy what running can do to you. This meant that ever so slightly I’d distribute some of my focus to the bike for the first 4 weeks of the build.

Newtons first law of motion seems to apply to both the body and the mind, I found solace in my volume, it was easier to train than to rest and circle. For a couple lucky weeks my life was entirely focused on keeping myself in motion, the implied ladder was not an option I wanted to see the results to. Late September provided some calm to my non running hours, dropping my hourly volume from 14 hours per week in the first 4 weeks of the build to 11. I started a new job and settled a couple of my qualms. I think those weeks are the happiest I have been in a long time. But as per historical trends, I am terrible at keeping a good thing going, and as per newton, I knew that I had to keep moving.

Racing used to be such a dreadful experience, I used to wake up in cold sweats from dreams of the pain and nerves. However this time around racing has been anything but. Traveling to Victoria with the closest thing I have to a family in this country, besides my brother, was an incredible experience. I spent my weekend laughing and smiling. Maybe it’s because I had ran out of frowns and tears in the weeks leading up to the race. In all honesty the race was anything but a success for most of my group. James, Matt, and I had our unique stomach problems each struggling, sometimes walking, and even puking throughout the course of the 21.1 kilometres. I failed to meet my completely arbitrary, meaningless, and slow compared to past accomplishments goal of going under 74 by nearly an entire minute. Despite this I couldn’t help but continue smiling after the race. Objectively I had failed. But the race and trip went great. I didn’t wake up in a cold sweat, I was nervous with excitement, and I wanted to hurt. I couldn’t help but smile when I ran past people who somehow knew my name, looking back to try and guess who it was. I waved at everyone I recognized from the last 5 years even if it hurt like a bitch to do so. I couldn’t help but jump up and down in joy when I saw my friend who had made the trip out to see us at the finish line. For a week I struggled with the conflicting feeling of failure and happiness. For so much of my running career I was defined by arbitrary goals, recently those goals weren’t even set by me, by extension my failure to reach them defined me. I have a hard time justifying the feeling of happiness despite failure, both in running and in other aspects of life.

Guilt is an interesting thing. It has been hard to separate success and happiness, and a consequence of setting high goals is a perpetual lack of success despite improvement. Thus any amount of happiness or success is accompanied by the guilt that I am not enough, not as a runner or a person to those I love. However this reconnection with running from a personal angle, some reading, and the experience in Victoria surrounded by my loved ones has lead me to attempt to separate the two. It is obvious that the journey is the true gift, as most runners know, however it’s easy to ignore that gift when the result of it shy of what you expect. It’s okay to have qualms, to use them to move forward and set direction, however impeding yourself from the emotion, whether good or bad, because of a result is disregarding the journey. It’s okay to be happy and have failed, as long as you can try your best not to fail in that way again. Thus I can look back at that weekend in Victoria with nothing but happiness. There may be a large cross on my wall where I wrote down my goals for that day, but that weekend was nothing short of excellent, I can allow myself to admit that. Thank you to all those I got to share it with. With the half in the past and just as much turbulence ahead, it seemed only natural to continue the build.

The following race was a local road 5k, nearly the furthest road discipline you can get from a half marathon. The training is intense, your muscles ache and lock in ways that the slower paces of longer distances could never cause. I hate when difficulty or accomplishment is attached to distance. To the blind eye a marathon or ironman is more impressive than a rainy road 5k or even more so than a 1500m around a track. Thats not to say that distance itself isn’t hard, it is, but it doesn’t take away from the intensity and pounding that can be poured into shorter events. At the end of the day its not the duration or time its the build up of effort and passion that lead up to the race, and are poured into it during the execution of it.

Finishing the planned road season I was left without a goal, I took my first off day in 70 days (The entire RVM build). Still troubled with myself and my current state I find that I have no choice but to continue in motion, continue with the direction I set myself in this June, before I knew any of the events, trouble, and change that would come with it. I want to delve even deeper into it, because the more I do the more I understand myself, my wants, and my flaws. The next goal is natural, return to Cross Country Nationals for the first time in 4 years, to give myself one last shot to compete while I’m a ‘collegiate’ and to continue moving forward. I am excited to delve deeper than I ever have into running, I hope to learn more about myself, my body, and life while running in the cold November rain.

With a continuous direction set for once in a long long time, I’ve been able to reflect on myself. The following are a series of observations that my return to being a ‘runner’ has allowed me to make.

Most of what I know about life, I know because of running. You cant ever really dwell on a run, much less a stride, once it’s gone. I allow myself to focus on it as it’s happening, to try to squeeze the most I can out of it, to devote myself whole in the act. But once it’s done, you have to do your best to leave it be. It’s okay to look back and wish you had pushed harder, backed off sooner, fondly reminisce, or spitefully analyze for a little while, but not any longer than that. If you do you’ll find yourself resting on your laurels or unable to get back up, both of which lead to eventual failure. The same can be said about people, phases, work, or experiences. It’s not easy to do this, not with life or with running. It’s easy to crutch yourself on the next run, person, or experience, by rushing towards it. But you must always find the time to recover between runs. I can’t say I’m the best at this, physically or metaphorically. But as I’ve grown older I’ve become much better at realizing when the run is done, even if it takes a couple times to really believe it. You also can’t define yourself by whatever a runs outcome was. Good or bad. The run itself, and yourself are both much bigger than the result. The race is always a lot more than the medal and personal record, the internship is much more than the return offer, the relationship is so much more than the break up. Sometimes I wish it was just that, just a conclusion I can forget about or a medal I can hang, but when I reflect on my running I seldom look fondly at the time and pace, rather the run (or runs) that lead me to such result.

Most of the parts of myself that I’m proud of, I am because of running. In the past few weeks I’ve tried to write down the things about myself that I love and hate, the thought exercise comes from an attempt to cut off the fat in order to make space for growth. When I look at the column on love I’m surprised by how nearly every single one of them only exists because of my running. It makes me question who I would be without it. ‘Passion’ ‘Grit’ ‘Devotion’ are not words I or those who knew me would’ve used to describe myself as a teenager, however they are core to who I am now however flawed that is. ‘Ability to hurt’ ‘Reflection’ ‘Competitiveness’ are essential to how I’ve gotten to where I am, however unsatisfied as I may be with it, I can’t even begin to imagine where I would be without them. However those are all things most runners would describe themselves as, they are nearly second nature in the act of lacing up your shoes and heading out the door as frequently as I and most others do.

Most of the parts of myself that I hate, are why I am not a better runner. The opposite column is filled with aspects of myself that nearly directly inhibit myself from being better, both competitively and spiritually in the act of running (and life). ‘Temper’ ‘Vices’ ‘Insecurity’ have clouded my routine, forcing mistakes that have nearly derailed my career more times than I can count. The same can be said about an ‘Inability to be alone’ ‘Pettiness’ Rudeness’ the list could go on forever. It’s often through said routine of running that I’m able to reflect, hide, or move on from my bad habits. Being too busy simply doesn’t allow for vice, enough volume is so loud that insecurities cant be heard, competition forces you to be alone, and to watch that temper. The deeper I dwell in the silence of my footsteps the more I understand myself and flaws, the more I learn and regret, and often, the more they disappear.

Most of what I want in life, I want because of running. Sometimes it’s hard for someone to say what they want in life, I’m not talking about material things or things out of their control, those are quite easy to come up with, rather pillars that will allow you to sit down when you’re old and fade without regret. Although ever changing and probably wrong, my pillars that allow me to sleep at night (or at least try to) heavily resemble or surround running. Freedom, flexibility and support to have time for myself. People I can share my passions with. Ability to support those I love emotionally and physically. Perpetual growth. So on and so forth.

I don’t write any of this pretentiously. I’ll be the first to admit that as a runner and athlete, I am lacking in talent and polluted by the self. In most cases the speed or ability with which you do something is irrelevant, at least to me. My father runs slower than nearly everyone I’ve had the pleasure of running with these last 8 years. His goals and mine are on complete opposites of the spectrum as far as ‘greatness’ is concerned in the world athletics ranking table. However my father is a better runner than most of the people I’ve ran with, and certainly a better runner than me. He runs with more passion and heart than most if not all, regardless of the fact most people would call his runs a ‘shuffle’. Running alike most endurance sports isn’t really about pace, it’s about heart and understanding. The luxury to feel the wind when I run and compete at the level that I’ve gotten to are a complete aside to what this reflection is about. I will always respect the passion, devotion, and understanding with which my father laces his shoes in the dark, before work, more than I will any time, result, or achievement anyone around me does. It just so happens that most of the people I have around me lace up their shoes in the same way my dad does, I hope to continue to lace mine the same way he and many of the runners I’ve shared kilometres with have.

This piece is dedicated to my dad, who gave me my first watch and taught me what it means to be a runner, along with everyone I’ve had the pleasure of running with, whether it be my high school teammates that knew me before I could break 25:00 in the 5k, the people who have out-kicked me for a podium spot at my biggest races, and everyone in between.
